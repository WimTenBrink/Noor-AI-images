# In Nature's Perfect Key: An Unfiltered Conversation with Noor
*Conducted by Dutch music journalist Senna Bakker in Abcoude, Netherlands*

The rise of the Utrecht-based vocal quartet **Noor**—comprising Miranda Noor (Soprano, bass), Annelies Brink (Alto, graphics), Fannie de Jong (Mezzo-Soprano, drums), and Emma Vermeer (Feminine Contralto, synths)—has captivated fans with their blend of modern electronics, traditional acoustic instrumentation, and sapphic themes. Yet behind their polished recordings lies a creative process that is unpredictable, chaotic, and spontaneous.

I visited the four singers at their Abcoude farm studio on a wet Tuesday afternoon. Outside, their goats roamed past the insulated barn doors; inside, the atmosphere was warm and slightly noisy. A tray of strong Dutch coffee—with sugar and milk set on the table—was laid out beside a plate of *Mokkaschuimgebakje*, a crisp Dutch mocha meringue pastry with airy baked egg-white layers, mocha-infused cream, and toasted hazelnut croquant. Between crumbling pastries, barking dogs, and sudden tangents, we settled into an unfiltered conversation about their music.

---

## Part 1: Studio Chaos and Asymmetrical Energy

**Senna Bakker:** Thanks for having me. Sitting in this space, there's a strong dynamic between the four of you. How does that translate when you're tracking in the room together?

**Fannie de Jong:** It's mostly just noise until somebody hits record, honestly. Miranda will be obsessing over a bass riff for forty minutes, and I'll just start banging on the floor-gong because my foot went to sleep—

**Miranda Noor:** —Because you refused to take a break! You were sitting on your leg for two hours.

**Fannie de Jong:** —And then Emma plays three chords on the Juno-106 and suddenly we're like, "Wait, is that a chorus?" Half of our tracks come from somebody messing up a rehearsal take or dropping a drumstick on the floor.

**Emma Vermeer:** *[Setting down her coffee cup]* We don't overthink the start. Miranda brings the words, Fannie brings the noise, and I try to find the key center before everyone starts arguing about tempo.

**Annelies Brink:** *[Looking up from sketching on her tablet]* I mostly just make sure nobody accidentally deletes the project files.

**Senna Bakker:** Annelies, you sit in the lower mid-range vocal register. Do you usually construct your harmony lines after Miranda lays down the main melody?

**Annelies Brink:** Sometimes. Or I just pick a note that sounds low enough so I don't have to strain my neck.

**Fannie de Jong:** *[Laughs]* Tell her about the synth patch! She hated it!

**Annelies Brink:** I did. In the second verse of our latest track, Emma used this buzzing, saw-wave synth patch that sounded like a vacuum cleaner in an empty hallway. I begged her to change it, but Fannie loved it, so it stayed. Now whenever we play it live, I just glare at Emma during that bar.

**Emma Vermeer:** It adds grit. Perfect synth tones are boring.

---

## Part 2: Lyrics, Clutter, and Spontaneous Quirks

**Senna Bakker:** Let me ask about the production details. On the bridge of your latest piece, there's a sudden foot-stomp and an abrupt key shift into F Minor right before the third chorus. What was the intellectual intention behind that sudden tonal shift?

**Miranda Noor:** Intellectual? *[Laughs]* Senna, honestly? We ran out of bars on the demo arrangement. We were trying to bridge the verse to the final section, and Fannie accidentally kicked the microphone stand while reaching for her mug. The tape caught the thud. We listened back and went, "Well, that thud actually marks the beat better than a click track." So we kept the thud and pitched the next chord up because Emma's synth key got stuck.

**Emma Vermeer:** The key didn't get stuck, Miranda. I just pressed the wrong key because your dog slammed against my knee.

**Miranda Noor:** Either way, it wasn't a masterclass decision. We just thought it sounded cool in the moment. People read way too much into that line about the 'rusted iron latch' as well. Critics wrote these long essays about industrial metaphors and societal pressure. Honestly, it just fit the rhythm of the vocal line. 'Iron latch' had the right consonants.

**Fannie de Jong:** *[Interrupting]* Although the line about the tea kettle was real! Remember when Emma tried to boil water in a plastic measuring cup?

**Emma Vermeer:** —We are not opening that story again in print, Fannie.

**Fannie de Jong:** There was smoke everywhere! The smoke alarm was going off for twenty minutes while Miranda was trying to record her high F5 operatic peak! That's why her voice sounds slightly breathless on the second take—she was literally inhaling melted plastic fumes!

**Annelies Brink:** *[Dryly]* Very romantic sapphic atmosphere.

---

## Part 3: Friction and Unpolished Workflows

**Senna Bakker:** That brings up an interesting point. Not everything on the track feels seamlessly slick. The tempo in the bridge feels slightly pushed—almost frantic—compared to the steady groove of the intro. Was that tension deliberate?

**Miranda Noor:** We actually had a huge argument about that. Fannie wanted to crank the tempo up to 124 BPM for the whole back half. I wanted to keep it grounded at 108.

**Fannie de Jong:** 108 was dragging! It felt like walking through wet cement!

**Miranda Noor:** It was subtle! But we were running out of studio time before the storm hit that evening, so we compromised on 114. If you listen closely, you can hear Fannie's hi-hat rushing the beat right before the chorus because she was determined to speed it up anyway.

**Emma Vermeer:** *[Reaching for a Mokkaschuimgebakje]* I still think the mix on the vocals is a bit too wet. The reverb tail drowns out Annelies' low alto notes in the third verse. If we had another day in mastering, I would have pulled the wet mix down five percent.

**Annelies Brink:** I liked the reverb. It hid the fact that I sang the wrong vowel on 'harbor'.

**Fannie de Jong:** *[Jumping up]* Hang on, I need to let the dog back in—he's scratching at the outer door. *[Fannie steps out for a moment; a faint bark echoes down the hallway before she returns and sits back down.]*

**Senna Bakker:** *[Laughing]* It seems like the charm of Noor is precisely this mix of high vocal precision and messy, unscripted studio reality.

**Miranda Noor:** If you polish out all the bumps, you lose the pulse of the room. We live together, we eat together, we fight over who forgot to restock the coffee, and that mess comes straight through the microphones.

## Part 4: Senna's Unfiltered Track Review

**Senna Bakker:** Having spent the last two hours listening to the raw stems and the final master in your control room, here is my honest assessment.

### Track Evaluation & Score Breakdown

| Criterion | Score (1-10) | Journalist Commentary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Vocal Quartet Synergy** | **9 / 10** | Flawless vocal blend. Miranda's high soprano and Annelies' low grounding alto complement each other naturally without feeling forced. |
| **Lyrical Subtext & Phrasing** | **8 / 10** | Strong, grounded lyricism. Unpretentious and evocative without falling into overly academic traps. |
| **Production & Grit** | **7 / 10** | Refreshingly unpolished in parts, though the bridge tempo rush and slightly heavy reverb in verse three reveal real studio friction. |
| **Overall Score** | **8.0 / 10** | A vibrant, living performance that thrives on its spontaneous quirks rather than hiding behind clinical digital perfection. |

### Detailed Review Notes
- **Highlights:** The acoustic resonance of the bowed Crwth against the Juno-106 pad creates an authentic, physical beating frequency. The vocal trade-offs between Fannie's staccato phrasing and Emma's velvety contralto give the middle section immense personality.
- **Flaws & Rough Edges:** The transition into the bridge remains noticeably awkward due to the abrupt foot-stomp thud and tempo acceleration. While it gives the track character, purists expecting a seamless symphonic shift might find it jarring.

## Part 5: Farewell at the Gate

**Senna Bakker:** Despite those minor rough edges—or perhaps because of them—the track feels genuinely alive. Thank you all for opening your studio doors and sharing the tea, coffee, and meringue pastries.

**Miranda Noor:** Thanks for coming out to Abcoude, Senna. And for not tearing us apart over that bridge tempo!

**Fannie de Jong:** Next time bring helmet gear, the wind on the canal road is brutal today!

**Emma Vermeer:** Watch out for the gravel near the gate when you start your motorcycle.

**Annelies Brink:** *[Waving dryly]* Don't forget your gloves on the table.

**Senna Bakker:** Got them! Take care, everyone.
